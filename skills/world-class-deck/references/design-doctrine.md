# Design doctrine

## Avoid the obvious AI deck

Common failure modes:

- dark navy + cyan/purple gradient with no subject rationale;
- three equal rounded cards on every slide;
- decorative gradient blobs behind unrelated content;
- section labels such as `01 / 02 / 03` that add no meaning;
- giant quotation marks or accent bars used as filler;
- every page centered and symmetrical;
- tiny explanatory text beneath oversized generic headings;
- fake product screenshots or fabricated UI.

## Build a visual language from the subject

Pick one or two source metaphors from the material itself: network topology, editorial photography, archival documents, industrial diagrams, field notes, code, maps, biological forms, financial ledgers, etc.

Translate that into:
- composition;
- image treatment;
- line/shape language;
- chart styling;
- transitions between sections.

## Hierarchy

The audience should know where to look first within roughly one second.

Use scale, whitespace, contrast, and placement before adding decoration.

## Typography

- Minimize type families.
- Prefer a clear scale with intentionally few sizes.
- Avoid shrinking below comfortable projection/viewing sizes.
- Shorten copy before shrinking text.

## Imagery

Crop intentionally. One strong image is usually better than a collage of weak stock images. Do not stretch images or hide critical labels beneath them.

## Charts

Strip non-essential ink. Highlight the comparison that proves the title. Do not color every series equally if only one matters.
